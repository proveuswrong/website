# Public Goods Funding

An application of [Decentralized Curation](/projects/decentralized-curation/): assessing public goods work in the open, and funding it by the assessment. It is a proposed design, set out in its own preprint.

## The Problem

Funding public goods means someone has to judge which work matters, and how much. This application would make that judgment contestable and revisable. Its reference question is narrow: keeping a credible, revisable assessment of work that is done and maintained. Whether another grant would do the most good is a different question, and needs its own policy and evidence.

## How It Would Work

In the proposed design, three operations stay separate:

1. **Contest factual claims.** Nominators would publish nominations of completed work, each with a deposit. Challengers would earn rewards for showing that a claim is false or breaks another stated rule. An external arbitrator would decide.
2. **Assess relevance.** Paid jurors would score each nomination against a public goal and rubric. The jury would not decide whether a factual claim is false.
3. **Use the assessment.** A stated rule would turn the scores into funding shares when a budget exists. Scores could exist before any budget does, and future shares could change without rewriting what was already earned.

## Limits

It is a proposed design and an evaluation agenda, not a completed experiment or a validated deployment, and it reports no study results. Whether honest answers are participants' best responses, whether competent jurors can be selected reliably, whether correction is affordable and whether public outcomes improve are open questions. A score is a policy choice: twice the weight does not mean twice the good.
